## 1. THE SHAPE OF THE RUN

### 1.1 The one idea after the splash: APERTURE

Today every scene after the fly-in is presented by TRAVEL — the camera pulls back to show the panes, turns the solid to show the decades, dollies into a room. The brief asks for a different method after the splash, and for the weight to sit on the reveal. The method proposed is that from the strike onward **nothing is travelled to; everything is opened onto**. Each later scene is first GLIMPSED through an opening in the scene on screen, the opening widens at one slow rate with the camera locked, and the opening becomes the frame. Five openings, in order:

1. **the front** — the invariant is seen behind the wave through the chaos (the calm is the first aperture);
2. **the centre of the solid** — as the icosahedron expands out into the 600-cell and E8, the first room is visible small at its centre, through every shell;
3. **each monitor's glass** — the next room, three times (the search re-expressed as a fall);
4. **the deepest glass** — black, with the bud in it;
5. **the petals' crack** — the answer's room, lit before it is visible.

Rules carried by every opening (the look dimension's "majestic vs kitsch" line, applied as mechanism): the aperture radius follows ONE ease family, `easeInOutPower(u, 3.5)` from `config/ease.js` (the rational form: 2.09 % of travel in the first quarter — the crack), so the five openings rhyme; the camera does not move while a thing is opening; one colour of light per beat; the thing inside is lit before it is seen (seam glow leads geometry); and the final opening is the slowest.

### 2.3 The compute simulation (resolving chaos vs stack: buffers on an icosphere, not a texture cube-sphere)

The chaos dimension built its CGL on an equiangular cube-sphere TEXTURE (6×208²); the stack dimension proposed an icosphere with `attributeArray` BUFFERS and a kernel that writes only its own element. The buffer form wins, for three reasons: (1) it runs on BOTH lanes — three's WebGL backend runs `.compute()` via transform feedback and reads storage buffers through PBO-backed textures, but has no `textureStore` path at all (the look dimension's finding; `lab/heat.js` cannot run on `?gl=1` for exactly this reason); (2) the icosphere IS the site's object — its symmetry group is the icosahedral group, Y₆ is exactly representable on it, and it has no poles or corners (the cube-sphere's corner metric is what made the researcher's dt unstable); (3) the mesh is the grid: the field is read in the vertex stage as an attribute and the GPU interpolates across triangles — no hand-written bilinear, no rgba32float filtering feature.

- Grid: icosphere V = 10·4ᵏ + 2; k = 6 → 40,962 vertices (phones and the CI lane), k = 7 → 163,842 (desktop). Mean cell h = R·√(4π/V): 0.0175 R at k = 6, 0.0088 R at k = 7.
- Equation: `A_t = μA + (1+ib)Δ_S A − (1+ic)|A|²A + γ·s·Y₆`, b = 0.5, c = −3 (1+bc = −0.5). μ = μ_hot(1−s) + μ_cold·s with μ_hot = 1, **μ_cold = −1.2** (raised from the sketch's −0.3 so the lock is near the linear response and completes within ~10 CGL units of the front), γ = 0.4 (linear lock amplitude γ/|μ_cold| = 0.33 at a cap; the drawn relief behind the front is normalised by |μ_cold|/γ so caps map to ±1). **Heat is render-only**: `T_t = κΔT − λT + Q·∂s/∂t`, κ = 0.6, λ = 0.5, Q = 0.7, and T does NOT enter μ (the verifier showed μ_T·T_max > |μ_cold| re-ignites the ring and delays order by 1.3–2.5 s).
- Radius: R = 18 CGL units (≈ 58 spirals on the sphere; ≈ 2.3 per radius). Work ∝ N² at fixed cells per CGL length; R = 25 costs 1.9× more cells. Prototype-gated.
- Laplacian: cotangent finite-volume, `ΔA_i = (1/𝒜_i) Σ_j w_ij (A_j − A_i)`, w_ij = (cot α_ij + cot β_ij)/2, baked per vertex (6 neighbours + 6 weights + area). dt from a MEASURED spectral radius ρ (power iteration on the CPU at build, ~50 iterations): `dt = 0.9 · 2 / (ρ (1+b²))`, and dt ≤ 0.05 from the cubic term (the sketch's finding). Expect ≈ 0.018 at k = 6 / R = 18 (≈ 5 steps per frame at tempo 6 units/s) and ≈ 0.0045 at k = 7 (≈ 21 per frame). Forward Euler, two buffers ping-ponged, each kernel invocation writing its own element only.
- The front is KINEMATIC, not simulated: θ(p) is a table integrated once offline from `R dθ/dt = c − (D_s/R) cot θ` with D_s/(cR) = 0.12 (θ_c = 6.8°, below the 8.6° cap that `frontFrom = 0.15` in hyperbolic radius already seeds: θ = asin(tanh 0.15)), retuned so the crossing takes the `front` window. Being a pure function of progress it keeps `?at=` exact and lets the ζ-rings, the heat pulse and the corners all read one number. Front speed at 9 s × [0, .5]: πR = 56.5 units over 27 CGL time units → v ≈ 2.1, the regime in which the moving-quench test locked (0.75 → 0.98 behind the front); the failed regime was v = 5.24.
- Checkpoints: the turbulence needs ~120 time units to develop (≈ 6,700 steps at k = 6) — far too many for a seek — so the state at `flyIn.eggIn` (0.38) and at `flyIn.strike` (0.93) is baked as a static asset (float32: 41k × 4 × 4 B = 656 KB at k = 6; 2.6 MB at k = 7; gzip halves it), keyed by a hash of kernel source + parameters + k, regenerated by `scripts/bake-cgl.mjs` whenever any of them changes. A live run steps from the eggIn checkpoint; a `?at=` pin restores the nearest earlier checkpoint and replays a fixed number of fixed-size steps.
- Prototype gate before porting to the site (mandatory — two of three of the researcher's extrapolations were wrong): corr(Re A, Y₆) > 0.9 within 10 CGL units behind the front at v ≈ 2.1; defect density at R = 18 readable; timestamp-query pass cost on a real desktop GPU and a phone.

### 2.6 What runs on the fallback and what degrades

Runs on WebGL2 (≈ one visitor in five or six, more on Android; all Safari on pre-26 OSes; Firefox on Linux/Android/Intel Macs): every TSL material, the backdrop nodes, the post stack (bloom/afterImage/film/rgbShift/CA verified pixel-identical across backends), the CGL via transform feedback (buffers only — no storage textures anywhere by design), the nest (stencil on the WebGL backend NOT yet probed — PR-8 probes; `ClippingGroup` is the fallback), the petals (no compute). Degrades: k = 6 grid and 5 steps/frame everywhere the adapter class says "phone or fallback"; bloom at resolutionScale 0.5; the Escher twist off. If transform-feedback CGL is too slow on a given device, the fallback of last resort is a baked field atlas at k = 5 (10,242 cells × 96 frames × fp16 ≈ 3.9 MB).

### 2.7 How "every frame is a pure function of progress" survives

Everything except the CGL state stays pure: the front θ(p), the corners, the shells, the wheel's L/R slerp, the nest's ζ, the petal angles, the landing frustum are all functions of p. The CGL is made a function of (checkpoint, step count): `seek(v)` = restore the nearest earlier checkpoint + N(v) fixed-size steps, deterministic on one device (SwiftShader gave identical readback hashes across launches and sessions). Cross-device, WGSL may reassociate arithmetic, so the chaotic phase is compared by STATISTICS (mean |A|², defect count, projection on Y₆ read back from the buffer), while the ordered phase behind the front is an attractor and diffs at the pixel level as before. The `gate` mechanism (holding `t`, advancing `elapsed`) is unchanged. Time-based post nodes are banned; `renderAsync()` is not used (deprecated r181).

## 3. THE MATHS

### 3.1 What "the Monster group" should mean in the piece

It cannot be literal: no oscillation settles into the Monster, and the Monster is not drawable (smallest faithful representation 196,883-dimensional; smallest permutation representation on ≈ 9.7×10¹⁹ points). What the surface settles into is the **icosahedral invariant** — the group of the object on screen, |I| = 60 (rotations, ≅ A₅), |I_h| = 120 (with inversion, ≅ A₅ × Z₂), binary cover 2I ≅ SL(2,5) of order 120 (NOT I_h). The Monster is the top of a ladder whose bottom rung is that group, and every rung up to E8 is drawn exactly; above E8 the piece carries NUMBERS, not pictures. State this in copy and in code comments as a ladder, never as an equation:
